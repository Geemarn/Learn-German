# B1 Grammatik: "Als" vs. "Wenn" (When to use which?)

In English, the word **"when"** is used for everything. But in German, you must choose between **"als"** and **"wenn"**. This is a very common B1 exam topic!

Here is the ultimate rule you need to remember:

## 1. ALS = A single event or block of time in the PAST
Use **als** only when you are talking about the past, AND the event only happened **one single time** or refers to a **single continuous block of time** (like your childhood).

**Trigger words for *als*:** gestern (yesterday), letztes Jahr (last year), damals (back then).

**Examples:**
*   **Als** ich ein Kind war, habe ich viel ferngesehen. 
    *(When I was a child, I watched a lot of TV. -> Childhood only happens once).*
*   **Als** ich gestern nach Hause kam, war die Tür offen.
    *(When I came home yesterday, the door was open. -> Happened exactly once yesterday).*
*   Ich war sehr glücklich, **als** ich die B1-Prüfung bestanden habe!
    *(I was very happy when I passed the B1 exam. -> You only pass it once).*

---

## 2. WENN = Present/Future OR Repeated events in the Past
Use **wenn** for everything happening **now (present)** or in the **future**. 
You also use **wenn** for the past, but ONLY if the event happened **multiple times** (repeatedly).

**Trigger words for *wenn*:** immer (always), jedes Mal (every time), oft (often), heute (today), morgen (tomorrow).

**Examples (Present / Future):**
*   **Wenn** ich Zeit habe, lerne ich Deutsch.
    *(When/If I have time, I learn German. -> Present).*
*   Ich rufe dich an, **wenn** ich morgen in Berlin ankomme.
    *(I will call you when I arrive in Berlin tomorrow. -> Future).*

**Examples (Repeated Past):**
*   **Immer wenn** ich meine Oma besuchte, gab es Kuchen.
    *(Whenever / Every time I visited my grandma, there was cake. -> Repeated action in the past).*
*   **Jedes Mal, wenn** es regnete, blieben wir zu Hause.
    *(Every time when it rained, we stayed home. -> Repeated action in the past).*

---

## 3. Side-by-Side Comparison (The Ultimate Test)

Look at these two sentences in the past. Notice how changing the word changes the entire meaning of the sentence!

**Sentence A:** **Als** ich in Berlin war, habe ich Currywurst gegessen.
*Meaning:* I went to Berlin exactly ONE time in my life, and during that trip, I ate Currywurst.

**Sentence B:** (Immer) **Wenn** ich in Berlin war, habe ich Currywurst gegessen.
*Meaning:* I traveled to Berlin MULTIPLE times in my life. And every single time I went there, I ate Currywurst.

---

## 4. Grammar Rule: Verb at the End!
Both *als* and *wenn* are subordinating conjunctions (Nebensatzkonjunktionen). This means they **always push the conjugated verb to the very end of the clause**.

*   Als ich ein Kind **war**, ... (Verb *war* at the end)
*   Wenn ich müde **bin**, ... (Verb *bin* at the end)

If you start the sentence with the *Als/Wenn* clause, the main clause immediately starts with the verb (Verb in Position 2 of the whole sentence):
*   [Als ich ein Kind war], **spielte** ich oft draußen.
